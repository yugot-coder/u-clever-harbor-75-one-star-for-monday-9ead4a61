## 11 — CHOSEN

(23 words, 118 chars)

## Why #11 wins
- Restaurant language throughout, mapped to Monday: booked a table, sat me (table),
  served (dish: cold coffee), billed me (bill) — hits judge criterion 2 with three
  of its four named props, where most rivals manage only one.
- Monday-specific twice over: the alarm (Monday morning's 6am alarm) and the weekend
  (the bill is for the weekend you just lost) — criterion 3, and the line could not
  be about any other weekday.
- Punchline lands last: "billed me for the weekend" is the quotable, screenshot-able
  twist — criterion 4. "One star" is left out on purpose: the judge already knows
  every entry is a one-star review, and the slot is better spent on the joke.
- 23 words / 118 chars: comfortably inside the 25-word / 180-char gate, short
  enough to retype into a chat without trimming.
- Avoids the generic "Monday is awful" cliché every other entry leans on; the
  complaint is concrete (a table, an alarm as a table neighbour, cold coffee, a bill).

## Why the others lose
- #1, #4, #8: real-review texture but the Monday joke is weak — swap in any bad day
  and the line still works.
- #2: close second ("charged me for the weekend I just had") but "9:47" eats words
  and the punchline repeats the same joke as #11 with less restaurant texture.
- #3, #6, #7: good images (alarm clock, paying for your own alarm) but wordier
  setups and a weaker final beat than "billed me for the weekend".
- #5: "kitchen is clearly still closed" is nice, but the 9am meeting is a weaker
  dish than cold coffee served at the table.
- #9: close third — "fresh start" + "bill for my weekend" — but "yesterday's
  leftovers" is a muddier image than being seated next to your alarm.
- #10: "kitchen is closed forever" overstates the complaint; a one-star review
  complains, it does not declare the place dead.
